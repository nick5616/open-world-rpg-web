# CR 028: Playable instruments (playtest 2)

Branch `pt2/instruments`. Nicole (a musician; "all sorts of good shit"): instruments you can actually play, that people near you hear.

## Summary

- **Four instruments** (`instrument:*` items, category `instrument`, kind tool): **Lute** (plucked), **Wooden Flute** (blown, held notes), **Hand Drum** (bass / tone / slap), **Handbells**. General stores sell the lute, flute and drum; tool merchants the handbells and a flute (content.yaml `instruments.wares`, appended after the WP20 stock). A flute, a drum and a lute are made at a camp workbench, handbells are cast at a forge.
- **Play mode**: **U** with an instrument in your pack (or "Play (U)" on the item in the inventory). The keyboard becomes four rows of a scale: Q W E R T ... start at the root, the number row is an octave up, A S D ... an octave down, Z X C ... two down. **Shift** raises a note an octave, **Space** accents it, the **mouse wheel** moves the keyboard by octaves, **← →** change the key, **↑ ↓** the scale (major, minor, pentatonic, minor pentatonic, dorian, mixolydian, phrygian, lydian, harmonic minor, blues, chromatic), **Tab** the instrument, **Enter** the metronome click, **Esc** puts it away. The overlay shows every key's note (the drum shows bass, tone and slap) with the roots in gold and the held keys lit; scale, key, octave, dynamics (p, mf, f) and tempo can also be clicked. Choices persist per browser (`lodestone.music.v1`). While playing, the player stands still and no other key reacts (the handler is installed before every panel's, like photo mode's). Releasing a movement key still reaches the body, so a held key never sticks. U is free outside a duel challenge (Y/U answer one; a pending challenge keeps U).
- **Sound**: every voice is synthesized, with no sample files. Nicole's libraries are genre loop packs (Novation Anniversary) and a Kontakt piano whose samples are encrypted and licensed for her own productions. None of them are single plucked, blown or struck notes, so synthesis was the right fit. The lute uses Karplus-Strong with the Jaffe-Smith extensions: a noise burst shaped by the pick and its position, an averaging loop filter, a per-pitch loss and all-pass fine tuning (within 12 cents in the tests). It has two strings per course a few cents apart and a body resonance. The flute is a near-sine with weak harmonics, breath noise band-passed at the pitch, a chiff, a scoop up to pitch and delayed vibrato, held while the key is down. The drum is a circular membrane's modes with a pitch drop; the register picks the stroke, and the note tunes the skin (it follows the scale like a tabla). The handbells are tuned-handbell partials (the fundamental and its twelfth, then inharmonic upper partials) as beating pairs with their own decays, plus a clapper transient. Struck notes are rendered into buffers once (two variants per note) and pre-rendered in idle slices when you pick the instrument up.
- **Acoustics**: your own instrument plays at once, centred, on the sfx bus. Other players' instruments are positional sources at their drawn bodies through the audio system's own rules (`audio/spatial.ts`): inverse distance easing to silence at `hearM` (40 m), air absorption, walls and rock between you (the engine's acoustic spaces) and equal-power direction. They also go through the engine's reverb send, so a song in a cave or a closed building rings there. The score ducks to 0.3 while an instrument sounds within 24 m. Voices are capped (16 local, 10 per player, 48 in all).
- **Avatars**: emote 22 (a looping system emote, value = instrument) poses the body: the lute across the belly with the right hand strumming, the flute at the lips, the drum at the waist with alternating hands (both for bass strokes), a handbell in each hand. Each note moves the hands (a strum, a strike, a ring, a breath), and the prop meshes show while playing. In first person the instrument is held up in the lower view with its own hands, and the weapon and arms step aside (`view.fpInstrument`).
- **Hooks**: **townsfolk applaud** a finished song: up to 6 NPCs within 20 m cheer or clap, sent as emote 3 on the NPC, at most once per 45 s per player. A **fireside song**: 30 s of playing at night within `fire.warmthM` of a burning campfire grants Rested with the fire's bonus to the player and to everyone by that fire, with no bedroll needed. **Feats**: Bard (100 notes, "the Bard"), Troubadour (2,000 notes), Crowd Pleaser (5 ovations), Fireside Tales (a fireside song). **Jam**: Enter sets a shared metronome (60-180 bpm); everyone playing within 40 m gets the same downbeat on the server clock and their click lines up.

## Wire format and budget

A note event is 16 bits: MIDI note (7), velocity (3; 0 = the note-off of a held flute note) and ms into its batch (6). The client sends the first note after a quiet spell at once. Notes in the next `batchMs` (50) ride one message: up to 5 events per Interact 120/2 (four in targetId, one in arg's low half, with the batch clock in its high half). That is at most 20 messages a second, and only while notes are played. The zone checks each note against the instrument's range and a per-player bucket of notes started (40, then 16 per second; the rest are dropped with one "too fast" per 2 s). It relays the batch as it arrives as one Event 1120 (about 35 bytes + 2 per note), only to players within 40 m. Interact 120 is not counted by the generic Interact limiter (10, then 5 per second, whose violations kick). It has its own bucket (12, then 25 per second), chosen on the frame's second byte in `readLoop`. Listeners rebuild the timing with a jitter buffer: the least recent latency plus a 45 ms margin keeps the player's spacing within and between batches. Nothing is sent per tick. A player who never plays costs nothing; one who plays costs a distance check per other player per batch, plus a once-a-second check. The scale scenario's bots never play, so `LODESTONE_WIRE_DIGEST=1` is unchanged.

## Server authority

Playing needs the item in the pack and the body on the ground (not downed, climbing, gliding or swimming: emotes' rule; not mounted: CR 027's riding bit). It stops the fishing line, and mounting ends the playing. The zone ends playing when the pose loop ends: walking 1 m away, another emote, a stop. Songs, applause, Rested and feats are the zone's; the client only reports notes.

## Known gaps

- Notes are relayed as played: a hostile client can play anything in range at up to the note rate, which is no worse than chat.
- Ghost-band players on a neighbouring zone (WP16) don't hear across the border; a handoff stops playing, and the client picks the instrument up again once.
- The NPC applause pose is the townsfolk's single-box arms (raised, or clapping in front).
- Remote players hear a note about 45 ms + their path's latency after it was played (the jitter margin); your own notes have no added latency.
- Names, numbers and tunings are proposals for Nicole (contracts/content.yaml `instruments`).
